Core teachings of the book

Four figures, four roles. Fiore marks every figure in the drawings. The crowned figure is a Magistro; the Remedy Master (Magistro Remedio), who waits in guard or makes the first cover, is crowned too. The Scolaro (scholar), who wears a golden garter below the knee, shows the plays that follow from his master's cover. The Contrario, wearing both crown and garter, is the counter to that remedy. The unmarked opponent is the Zugadore (player).

One counter breaks every scholar. According to Fiore, a counter made against a Remedy Master also breaks the plays of all his scholars. This holds for the spear, the axe, the sword, the dagger and Abrazare, in other words for the whole art.

Zogho Largo and Zogho Stretto. If the swords cross near the points, you are in Zogho Largo (the wide play) and work at distance with cuts and thrusts. If they cross at the middle of the blades, you are in Zogho Stretto (the narrow play), where grips, pommel strikes, locks, disarms and throws come in.

Four virtues. The Segno at the start of the book shows four virtues as four animals: Prudentia (the lynx), Celeritas (the tiger), Audacia (the lion) and Fortitudo (the elephant). A good fighter carries all four together.

Turns and steps. There are three turns: volta stabile (a turn in place), mezza volta (changing sides with one step) and tutta volta (one foot circling around the other). There are also four movements: passare (passing forward), tornare (passing back), acrescere (advancing) and discrescere (withdrawing).

One art, many weapons. The same guards return with every weapon. Porta di Ferro is used in Abrazare, with the dagger, the sword, the axe and the spear; the dagger remedies reappear in the Zogho Stretto of the sword.

The four virtues Segno

Four animals from the seven swords figure at the start of the book: the qualities a good fighter must carry.

4

In Fiore's Segno, seven swords and four animals surround a fighter. The swords stand for the seven blows, the animals for the four virtues needed in a fight.

Prudentia

Prudence

The lynx: no creature sees better; it moves only after careful calculation. It means reading distance, timing and the opponent's intent in advance.

Celeritas

Celerity

The tiger: it runs and turns so fast that even a thunderbolt cannot catch it. It is the speed of moving from cover to strike.

Getty · p. 41

Audacia

Audacity

The lion: no heart is bolder; it welcomes everyone to battle. It means going to the opening without fear.

Getty · p. 42

Fortitudo

Fortitude

The elephant: it carries a castle on its back, yet never falls to its knees or loses its footing. It is a firm stance and endurance.

IIAbrazare and Bastoncello 24 drawings

Abrazare guards Poste

The four guards of grappling.

4

Fiore advises judging whether the opponent is stronger, bigger or younger before grappling. What grappling needs is strength, speed, good grips, breaks, locks and dislocations, and strikes to the body's weak points. All four guards lead into the same first play.

Posta Longa

The Extended Guard

The long guard waits with the arms extended. At the first grip attempt it slips the right arm under the opponent's left and goes into the first play, taking him down with a lock.

The boar's tusk breaks the opponent's grip from below, then moves into Porta di Ferro to force him down. If that fails it turns to breaks, binds and dislocations.

The Iron Gate

The iron gate waits without moving. Fiore stresses that this guard serves not only in grappling but also with the spear, axe, sword and dagger.

The Guard of the Headband

The forehead guard lets the opponent take hold, then leaves the guard, brings him down into Porta di Ferro and finishes him with locks.

Abrazare plays Zogho de l'abrazare

The grappling plays of the Remedy Master and his scholars, with their counters.

16

First and second play. Press into the inside of the opponent's right elbow with your left hand, put your right hand behind his left elbow and turn your body to the left: he either goes down or his arm is dislocated. If he takes his left hand off your shoulder, seize his left leg and throat and throw him on his back (the third play).

The other scholars. A grip at the throat with a lift under the knee, thumb pressure under the ear, the Gambarola (a throw over the leg), a knee to the groin and finishing holds from behind. Most are followed at once by their counter: pushing the elbow up to turn the opponent.

Fiore's warning. The plays will not work in every situation. If you do not have a good grip, look for one at once so the opponent gains no advantage.

Bastoncello The short stick

A neck bind with a short stick, a lift between the legs, and the dagger remedies done with a stick.

4

The short stick wraps the opponent's neck in a strong bind; against an armoured opponent it is better to pass the stick between his legs and turn him on his back. The covers made with the stick are those of the Sixth and Eighth Remedy Masters of the dagger, and the same can be done with a hood or a rope. In the Getty the texts of these two drawings appear to be swapped.

IIIDaga (dagger) 86 drawings

The five dagger masters Cinque cose

Masters representing the five things to do against the dagger.

5

Against the dangerous dagger, arms, hands and elbows must move quickly to do one of five things: take the dagger, strike, break the arm, bind the arm, and put the opponent on the ground. Each master carries one of these as his emblem: a dagger in hand (the disarm), broken arms (the break), keys (the lock), a man under his feet (the throw) and the palm of victory.

Dagger guards Poste

Five guards: some are good in armour, some without, some in both.

5

Fiore sorts the dagger guards by how they work with armour. Single-handed (scempia) guards can cover long, so they also work unarmoured; doubled or crossed-arm (incrosada) guards are very strong in armour but cannot cover long.

The single low iron gate. Good in and out of armour; it covers with or without a dagger, defending with or without closing to grapple.

Middle Iron Gate (Double)

Full Iron Gate (Double, Crossed)

The crossed low iron gate. In armour it is like a fortress, but unarmoured it falls short because it cannot cover long.

First Remedy Master Primo Magistro

A one-handed cover against the overhead dagger; disarms, binds and throws.

21

The remedy. The master meets the overhead dagger with his left hand on the wrist and, turning his hand to the left, makes the dagger fall to the ground. Fiore says there is no better play.

The scholars. Ligadura mezana (the middle bind), a lift under the knee into a throw, a third play with no counter, a strong two-armed cover and breaking the arm over the shoulder. Pushing the point up near the elbow to take the dagger also belongs to this master.

The counters. Turning the dagger around the arm, seizing the left hand of the one who covers, and pushing the elbow to turn him. Fiore warns: woe to the remedy that lets its left hand be seized.

Second Remedy Master Secondo Magistro

A cover with crossed arms and the chiave di sopra (upper key).

4

The master covers with crossed arms and can apply all the earlier remedies from here; in armour there is no stronger cover, and he can cross to the right and left, from below and above. His scholar uses the chiave di sopra to break the arm, throw the opponent or take the dagger. The counter is to push the elbow, turn him and strike.

Third Remedy Master Terzo Magistro

Plays against reverse (roverso) blows and the chiave di sotto.

7

This master meets roverso, the reverse dagger blows. His scholars drive the opponent into the ground, dislocate the arm and put him into the chiave di sotto, the lower lock Fiore calls the “strong key”: no one escapes this bind, and every weak point can be struck, armoured or not. Its counter is itself a strong bind.

Fourth Remedy Master Quarto Magistro

Upper binds, disarms and throws from a two-handed grip.

8

The master plays by seizing the opponent's arm with both hands. The scholars lock the arm with two different upper binds, throw by taking the elbow and the back of the knee, and take the dagger by turning it upward or sideways near the elbow. Against the upper bind the opponent can grip his right hand with his left, making his grip strong and breaking the lock. The counter ruins both scholar's and master's hands with one sharp wrench.

Fifth Remedy Master Quinto Magistro

Defending against a collar grab; arm breaks and locks.

12

As the opponent grabs your collar and is about to strike with the dagger, the master ruins his arm; the collar grab actually helps the defender, as long as he moves quickly. The scholars wait with crossed arms and put him into the middle or lower lock, dislocate the arm over the shoulder, and step behind to throw him. To finish, the dagger is turned and the point driven into the chest. The counter is to pull the arm in and bend it.

Sixth Remedy Master Sesto Magistro

A cover against blows from every direction; mezza volta and the elbow push.

8

This cover is good in and out of armour; it meets blows from every direction and leads into all the grips and binds. The scholars make a mezza volta from the cover to strike the chest, pass an arm over his arm and turn outward, take the dagger with a turn and put him into the lower lock. The counter is the elbow push: pushing the elbow to turn him, which Fiore finds good against every grip of the close play.

Seventh Remedy Master Settimo Magistro

A crossed-arm cover; the elbow push counter.

2

The master covers with crossed arms; this cover is better in armour and leads into the plays of the earlier masters. The counter is again a push to the right elbow, which Fiore says works against all close plays of the dagger, axe and sword, armoured or not. Strike the shoulder right after.

Eighth Remedy Master Ottavo Magistro

Covering by crossing with the dagger; moving into the strong key.

4

The master meets the attack by crossing with his own dagger; this cover is good in and out of armour. Some of his plays are shown with the earlier masters, some after him. From the crossed-arm cover the opponent is quickly put into the lower lock, the strong key. The counter is to turn him with a hard push to the elbow and strike obliquely, or to throw an arm around his neck.

Ninth Remedy Master Nono Magistro

Without a dagger; a grip against the low attack and what follows.

10

The Ninth King has no dagger. His grip resembles the Fourth Master's but is made against the low attack instead of the high one, and his plays differ. The scholars turn the dagger upward near the elbow and drive the point into the face, slip under the arm to dislocate it, reach between the legs to seize the hand, pass behind and bring the opponent down. Finally a scholar either draws his own sheathed dagger or takes the opponent's.

IVSpada (sword) 89 drawings

Sword against dagger Spada contra daga

Plays with a sword against a dagger, and with a sheathed sword against a dagger-wielder who grabs the collar.

9

With the dagger against the sword. The master waits in Dente di Zenghiaro and passes back with his right foot as he beats the sword aside; for one who knows Zogho Stretto this cannot fail. After the cover, strike the face or chest at once: with a dagger against a sword you must always close in.

With a sheathed sword. The dagger man grabs the collar and says “I will strike before you draw.” The swordsman jams the dagger arm with the scabbard, or lifts the sheathed sword under the dagger with the point toward the face while passing back, then draws and strikes.

Sword in one hand Spada a un man

The one-handed sword against three opponents; cover, disarm and the elbow push.

13

Three opponents want to kill the master: one will come with a thrust, one with a cut, and the third will throw his sword like a spear. The master says he will meet them one by one from his strong guard. The scholars step off the line with a strong cover, strike the opening with a thrust or cut, take the opponent's sword with the left hand, push the elbow to turn him and wrap the sword around his neck. Beating the thrust to the ground is also a core move here.

Two-handed sword: masters in guard Magistri in posta

Pairs of similar guards that counter each other; turns and steps.

8

Similar guards. Guards that resemble each other are counters to each other. The exception is the guards that stand ready to thrust (Posta Longa, Posta Breve and Mezana Porta di Ferro): when thrust meets thrust, the weapon with the longer reach strikes first.

Turns. From every guard you can make a volta stabile or a mezza volta, and the sword makes the same three turns. The four movements are passare, tornare, acrescere and discrescere.

Against thrown weapons. A group of six guards plays against thrown swords and spears: throwing the sword, beating aside what is thrown, a long thrust with extended grip, armoured half-sword play with the left hand at mid-blade, and holding the sword reversed to use it like an axe.

Colpi: cuts and thrusts Fendenti, Sottani, Mezani, Punte

The sword's seven blows: two fendenti, two sottani, two mezani and the punta.

4

The seven swords of the Segno are these seven blows. Every cut returns to guard along its own path and is used to break the other guards.

Fendenti

Downward Blows

The downward cuts: they cut from the teeth to the knee, end in one of the low guards and are very effective at breaking guards. They strike fast and return the way they came.

Getty 23r-a · p. 254

Sottani

Under Blows

The rising cuts: from the knee to the middle of the forehead, up the fendente's path. They come back down the same way or stay high in Posta Longa.

Getty 23r-b · p. 255

Mezani

Middle Blows

The middle cuts: they cross the paths of the fendenti and sottani through the middle. From the right they strike with the true edge, from the left with the false edge, anywhere between knee and head.

Getty 23r-c · p. 256

Punte

Thrusts

The thrusts: their target is the centre line between groin and forehead. They are made five ways: from the two high guards, the two low guards, and one from centre-line guards such as Mezana Porta di Ferro, Posta Breve or Posta Longa.

Getty 23r-d · p. 257

The twelve guards Le dodici poste

The twelve guards of the two-handed sword: each one's strength and job.

12

Fiore also divides the guards into still (stabile) and moving (instabile): some wait, some keep shifting into other guards to deceive. The “Powerful / Stable / Fluid” notes in the drawings reflect this classification.

Tutta Porta di Ferro

The Iron Gate (Powerful)

The low, very strong iron gate: with a good sword that is not too long it awaits every hand weapon. It passes with a cover into Zogho Stretto, exchanges thrusts, or beats the thrust to the ground while stepping.

Posta di Donna (right)

The Stance of the Queen on the Right (Powerful)

The Lady's guard on the right: it throws and covers all seven blows, breaks other guards with strong cuts and quickly exchanges thrusts. Step the front foot off line, then pass diagonally with the rear foot.

Posta di Finestra

The Stance of the Window (Fluid)

The window guard: quick, skilful and deceptive. A master of cover and strike, it threatens high and low guards alike and is excellent for strong thrusts, breaking the thrust and exchanging points.

Getty 23v-c · p. 260

Posta di Donna (left)

The Stance of the Queen on the Left (Powerful)

The Lady's guard on the left: always ready to cover or strike, it delivers strong blows, easily breaks the thrust to the ground and, with its traversing steps, enters Zogho Stretto quickly.

Getty 23v-d · p. 261

Posta Longa

The Extended Stance (Fluid)

The long guard is full of deception. It probes the guards, strikes with a thrust when needed, avoids the incoming cuts and answers with its own. It uses deception more than any other guard.

Getty 24r-a · p. 262

Mezana Porta di Ferro

The Middle Iron Gate (Stable)

The middle iron gate: strong because it stands in the middle, but it wants a long sword. It throws strong thrusts, beats swords upward and returns with a fendente to the head or arms.

Getty 24r-b · p. 263

Posta Breve

The Shortened Stance (Stable)

The short guard works better with a longer sword. It is deceptive but risky to wait in; it keeps moving, looking for a chance to enter with a thrust and a step. Better in armour.

Getty 24r-c · p. 264

Dente di Zenghiaro

The Wild Boar’s Tusk (Stable)

The boar's tusk strikes the way a wild boar does. It drives strong thrusts up into the face and comes back with a fendente to the arms, or repeats the thrust advancing the front foot. It defends well against Zogho Stretto.

Getty 24r-d · p. 265

Posta di Coda Longa

The Stance of the Long Tail (Stable)

The long tail: the sword stretches behind toward the ground. It can thrust, or step forward to cover and strike; passing with a fendente it easily enters Zogho Stretto. Good for waiting, since it moves quickly into other guards.

Getty 24v-a · p. 266

Posta Bicorna

The Two-Horned Stance (Fluid)

The two-horned guard is locked so firmly that its point never leaves the centre line. It can do everything Posta Longa does.

Posta Frontale (Corona)

The Headband Stance called the Crown (Fluid)

The forehead guard, called the crown by some masters: very good for crossing swords and against thrusts. It crosses a high thrust while stepping off line, breaks a low one to the ground, or passes back with a fendente ending in Dente di Zenghiaro.

Getty 24v-c · p. 268

Dente di Zenghiaro mezano

The Stance of the Boar’s Tusk in the Middle (Fluid)

The middle boar's tusk: the boar has two tusks, one low and this one on the centre line. It does everything the low one does, striking diagonally to displace the opponent's sword and thrusting at the opened opponent.

Zogho Largo The wide play

Plays from a crossing near the points: the Colpo di villano, the exchange of thrusts, breaking the thrust and the punta falsa.

20

First and second master. Crossed at the points, switch quickly to the other side and throw a fendente to the head or arms, or thrust to the face. Crossed at the middle of the swords, slide your blade over his hands, or pass with the right foot and thrust to the chest; seizing his sword and stamping on his knee belong to this master's scholars too.

Colpo di villano. Wait in a narrow stance with the left foot forward. When the peasant strikes, step off the line to the left, take the blow on the middle of your sword and let it slide to the ground, then answer with a fendente or a thrust to the chest. It also works against the axe and the staff. Against a cut to the leg, pass back and strike his head; Fiore finds cutting below the knee with a two-handed sword dangerous.

Scambiar di punta and rompere di punta. When he thrusts, step off line with the front foot and cross his sword with hands low and point high, thrusting to his face: this is the exchange of thrusts. Going with hands high and beating the thrust to the ground at mid-blade is breaking the thrust; then stamp on his sword, strike the throat with the false edge or close in.

Punta falsa. Feign a strong mezano to the head, hit his cover lightly, turn your sword to the other side of his blade and, gripping mid-blade with the left hand, thrust to the throat. Better in armour. Its counter is to make the same turn while stepping left onto his open side.

Zogho Stretto The narrow play

Plays from a crossing at mid-blade: grips, pommel strikes, locks and throws.

18

Everything from the crossing. In Zogho Stretto Fiore shows every kind of cover, strike, lock, dislocation, disarm and throw, with the remedies and counters for attacker and defender. Either side can make all the plays from the same crossing.

Fourth and fifth master. Pass with the left foot and seize his grip between his hands with your left hand, then strike with edge or point; take his elbow and hit his face with the pommel; wrap the sword around his neck and throw. If he covers from the right, seize his sword with the left hand; if from the left, take his left hand with the pommel and pull it up and back.

Sixth and seventh master. Put your hilt between his hands, push his arms up, throw your left arm over them to make a middle bind and strike repeatedly. Passing with a cover to strike the arms and face, or holding him by the hilt, also come from here; that grip breaks all the disarms. The First Dagger Master's play is repeated here with the sword.

Tor di spada Disarms

The upper, middle and lower disarms.

5

In the upper disarm you pin his hands with your left hand and press forward against his blade with your own grip until his sword falls. The middle one uses the same rotation with a different grip on the arm. In the lower disarm your right hand pushes his blade forward so his grip turns upward, your left hand staying on the turning grip. At close crossing you can also take his blade at mid-length, drop your own sword and turn his under the pommel.

Sword against thrown weapons Spada contra lanza

Three opponents: a thrown sword, a cut or thrust, and two spears.

2

The master waits with the two-handed sword in Dente di Zenghiaro; the left Posta di Donna or Posta di Finestra would serve as well. He steps his front foot off the line, steps diagonally with the rear foot and beats the incoming weapon toward the opponent's left: first the cover, then the counterattack at once.

Armoured sword guards Spada in arme

The six guards of armoured half-sword fighting.

6

In armour the sword is held at the half-sword and the thrust is the main weapon: the point goes to the gaps in the armour (face, armpit, gauntlet). Fiore's rule is short: step, cross and strike.

Armoured sword plays Remedy, scholar and counter

Cover from the Vera Croce, thrusts to the face, locks, pommel and crossguard strikes; the dueling swords.

18

Remedy and scholars. The Remedy Master covers from the Vera Croce by stepping diagonally off line. The scholars thrust low-hilted with point high into the face or chest, lift the visor if the thrust cannot pierce, push the elbow to strike from behind, or switch to grappling and put the opponent into the strong key. When the sword will not bite, strike the face with the crossguard or pommel, wrap the sword around the neck and step behind to throw.

The counter. During the cover the Counter-Remedy Master puts his left hand behind the opponent's right elbow, pushes hard, turns him and strikes under the arm, under the coif, behind the knee or wherever is left open. Fiore repeats that this rule holds for the whole art.

The dueling swords. The first serves as both sword and axe: unsharpened from the crossguard to a hand's width from the point, with a sliding roundel and a heavy spiked pommel. The second is sharp from hilt to point except for a hand's width in the middle for the gloved hand.

Axe guards Azza in arme

The six guards of armoured axe (poleaxe) fighting.

6

The axe guards share their names with the sword guards and are paired against each other: Posta di Donna plays against Dente di Zenghiaro, Coda Longa against Posta di Finestra. The whole art rests on covering by crossing weapons.

Posta Breve la Serpentina

The Stance of the Shortened Serpent

The short serpent delivers a powerful thrust that can pierce breastplates and cuirasses.

The Stance of the True Cross

The true cross defends by crossing weapons; for Fiore all of fencing and armed combat rests on the crossing cover. With a step and a thrust it repeats the armoured sword scholar's play with the axe.

The Stance of the Queen

The Lady's guard goes against Dente di Zenghiaro: stepping off line with the left foot and passing, it strikes hard at the head; blocked strongly from below, it hits the arms or hands instead.

Axe plays Remedy and scholars

Plays made after beating the opponent's axe to the ground.

10

Whoever beats the opponent's axe to the ground can make these plays. The scholars put the axe between his legs and cover his eyes with the left hand, step on his axe head and drive their own into his face, lift the visor to thrust inside, take him into the strong lower bind, wrench his axe away with a mezza volta and strike his head, and drag him down by the visor. The second master drags the opponent with the tail of the axe; the third carries a hollow axe filled with a blinding powder, which Fiore says he shows only for the sake of knowledge.

Staff and dagger against spears Lanza contra bastone

A staff and dagger, or two cudgels, against spearmen.

5

The master waits for two spearmen with a dagger in his right hand and a staff held upright in his left; tilting the staff to the right like Tutta Porta di Ferro, he turns without moving his feet and strikes with the dagger. If the spearmen poke with the butt and turn the spear around, they make the counter. The second master comes with two cudgels: one strikes the head, the other covers the spear, and the dagger goes into the chest. Had the spearman turned his spear under the dagger, he could have stopped him.

Lanza The spear

Three spear guards on the right, three on the left, and their shared finish.

12

The spear guards are close relatives of the sword guards. On the right side are Tutta Porta di Ferro, Mezana Porta di Ferro and Posta di Finestra: with a crossing step they beat the opponent's spear off line and enter with a thrust; stepping and beating aside together are hard to defeat. On the left are Dente di Zenghiaro, Vera Croce and the left Posta di Finestra; they too beat aside first, then thrust. All share one counter: when your spear is driven off line, turn it and strike with the steel-capped butt.

Ghiavarina against riders Lanza contra cavallo

The master on foot, in Dente di Zenghiaro, against three mounted spearmen.

4

Three riders want to kill the master: one will strike underhand, one will couch his lance at full reach, one will throw; each has one strike in turn. The master waits with a ghiavarina in Dente di Zenghiaro, steps off line with the right foot and crosswise with the left, and beats the spear aside; the same defence works with a staff or a sword. The counter is again to strike with the butt of the spear.

VIA cavallo (mounted) 44 drawings

Lance on horseback Lanza a cavallo

Mounted lance guards, the sword against the lance, and their counters.

17

A rider with a shorter lance but good armour carries it in Dente di Zenghiaro and lifts the opponent's lance diagonally off line; the lances cross an arm's length behind the points and the short lance runs home. The counter: if the long lance is carried just as low, the short one cannot beat it aside. With the left Posta di Donna a thrown spear is beaten aside; an unarmoured rider on a fast horse throws his lance point behind him as he flees. A sword against the lance beats it outward when passing on the opponent's right; the counter is to strike the horse's head or chest with a lance held low.

Sword on horseback Spada a cavallo

Beating aside from Coda Longa, disarms and a chain of counters.

16

Passing on the opponent's right, Posta di Coda Longa is very good against the lance and every hand weapon. Thrusts and blows from the left are beaten aside diagonally upward and outward, not straight up. After the beat the scholars thrust to the chest or face, cut the unarmoured head, seize the sword with the left hand, lift his hilt with theirs to make the sword fall, or throw an arm round the neck to bring him down. The counters follow one another: a strike to the leg, a pommel to the face followed by a horizontal backhand to the back of the head, and the hilt position that counters that. Fiore ends the mounted sword plays here and asks whoever knows more to share it.

Grappling on horseback Abrazare a cavallo

Mounted grappling, throwing the horse, taking the reins and the end of the book.

11

Catch a fleeing opponent on his left, take the cheek of his bascinet or his hair and pull him over backwards; the counter is to switch hands on the reins and lock him with the left arm. Lifting him by the stirrup or ankle, throwing the right arm over his horse's neck to wrench the bit up and over and bring down horse and rider, and taking the reins from his hands are here too; each is countered by an arm around the opponent's neck. In the special play the master catches a rider fleeing to his castle and strikes the armpit, where armour cannot protect. The book ends with Fiore's words that he has written the whole art here under the name Fior di Battaglia.
